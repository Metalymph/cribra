# Cribra v0.7 P0.3 incremental phase profile — historical investigation

Diagnostic-only, measured 2026-10-13 on `feature/v0.7-performance`.
During this investigation, no production algorithm, public library API, dependency,
or Criterion benchmark was changed. Nothing was committed, pushed, tagged, or
published. Temporary instrumentation has since been removed; the numerical
results are retained as the evidence behind the initial normalization target.

## Historical methodology

The temporary private diagnostic executable was removed on 2026-10-14 after
P0.3. Its profiling commands are no longer available. The measurements below
precede the retained P1.1 literal-only heap merge and P2.1 redundant old-source
check removal; they describe the investigated implementation, not current
performance or current code structure.

The investigation used Cargo's default optimized bench profile (opt-level 3,
no LTO, default codegen units), with `parallel` and `serde` plus temporary
instrumentation. Toolchain: rustc 1.99.0 (b940084d7 2026-09-28),
Cargo 1.99.0 (5f94df478 2026-08-27). The private executable compiled the same
source modules without `cfg(test)` and ran without concurrent compilation/tests.

Five warmups are discarded, then 51 fresh independent sessions are measured.
Session construction, source cloning for construction, compilation of rules,
reference full scanning, parity assertions, diagnostic collection, printing,
and destruction of the final session are outside the timed public edit.
The old snapshot destruction is inside the timed commit.

The fixture is exactly 65,536 ASCII `a` bytes, eight distinct rule IDs with
identical `aa` literals, and replacement of 32768..32769 by `x`.
Initial report/cache count is 524,280; post-edit count is 524,264.
Every runner iteration asserts exact full-scan report parity, source and revision.

## Normalization sorting and retention

The investigation preserved the total normalization timer and added nested timers
inside `normalize_candidates_by` enclose `sort_unstable_by` and the retention
state initialization plus `retain`, respectively. Comparator and retention
predicate bodies are unchanged; there are no timers inside either loop.

Both percentages below use ratios of medians from the same 51 measured edits:
phase median / total normalization median, and phase median / public edit median.
The nested rows are already included in total normalization and must not be
added again to the seven phase totals. The boundary residual is calculated as
normalization minus sorting minus retention for each iteration before taking
its median; it includes timer collection and call/boundary overhead. Independent
medians explain why sorting and retention percentages do not sum exactly to
100%, even after including the tiny boundary residual.

```csv
normalization_metric,median_ms,percent_of_normalization,percent_of_public_total
normalization_total,29.754625,100.000,77.717
normalization_sort,28.942667,97.271,75.597
normalization_retention,0.785750,2.641,2.052

```

The original unsplit run measured normalization at 29.338667 ms and public edit
at 37.761750 ms. This extension preserves both timing boundaries and methodology;
the new absolute timings include normal run-to-run variation and extra boundary
timer overhead. This difference is not evidence of a production regression.

## Boundaries and residual accounting

- Cache transfer includes invariant checks, cache/container allocation, ownership
  interval calculations, emission validation, mapping, context validation or
  reuse, and resource/work accounting. The timer encloses the complete transfer
  block, not individual records.
- Rescan/fallback includes regional literal enumeration, whole-rule fallback,
  ingestion and accounting, and ownership-buffer release. This literal-only
  fixture has no fallback rules, so no separate nonzero fallback cost is inferred.
- Literal sorting encloses all per-rule literal cache sorts.
- Projection includes both accepted counting and collection plus reservation.
- Normalization encloses the existing `normalize_candidates` call, including
  sorting and retention. Nested sorting and retention timers isolate those
  blocks as described above; the total boundary remains unchanged.
- Finding materialization includes result/storage accounting, vector allocation,
  position advancement, creation of all findings, destruction of the consumed
  accepted vector, and release of its payload accounting.
- Remaining report finalization includes structural cache cloning/accounting,
  suppression, boxed conversions, and report creation. Structural candidates
  are empty in this fixture.
- `Snapshot::update` includes `Snapshot::assemble`. Update's residual excluding
  assemble covers structural transfer/rescan/sort/validation and boundary overhead.
- Assemble's residual covers structural materialization, accepted-byte preflight
  outside normalization, snapshot construction, and boundary overhead.
- Preflight/staging includes public error validation, revision/length checks,
  baseline admission/resource accounting, and construction of the edited source.
- Commit includes publishing the staged snapshot and destruction of the old
  source, caches, and report. Their costs cannot be separated reliably without
  changing the production commit path; they are deliberately measured together.
- The time outside the seven requested phases is 0.619834 ms (1.619%). Staging
  and commit explain most of it. The remaining unattributed median after those
  two is 0.002750 ms (0.007%): update/assemble residual work, timer collection,
  boundary gaps, operation teardown, return, and caller measurement overhead.

Timers use `Instant` only at block boundaries and accumulate into a fixed-size
thread-local duration array. No diagnostic allocation or printing occurs in
matching, cache-transfer, projection, normalization, or materialization loops.
During P0.3, all instrumentation was conditional; it has now been removed.
There is small timing/collection overhead and ordinary machine/allocator noise;
sub-microsecond rows should not be interpreted as precise throughput estimates.
This is a wall-clock phase diagnostic, not a fresh Criterion baseline estimate.
The public median (38.285667 ms) differs from the supplied 35.477 ms baseline;
that difference alone does not establish a regression or instrumentation cost.

## Historical correctness evidence

The sorting/retention investigation passed 35 incremental and 16 scanner focused
tests with instrumentation enabled, and 34 incremental tests without it. The dense
check compared reports and all pre-normalization cache records/order against
replay, and checked determinism across independent sessions. Existing focused
tests covered UTF-8, error precedence, transactional snapshot preservation,
resource accounting, overlaps, rejected records, validators and fallback behavior.
The full test suite and Criterion suite were not run.

The dense report/cache/determinism check is retained as an unconditional permanent
test after profiling cleanup. All permanent parity, accounting and transactional
tests remain in place.

## Historical optimization finding

Candidate normalization accounted for 29.754625 ms, 77.717% of the complete public
edit. Sorting alone took 28.942667 ms: 97.271% of normalization and 75.597% of the
public edit. Retention took 0.785750 ms: 2.641% and 2.052%, respectively.

These measurements identified global accepted-candidate sorting as the first
optimization target, ahead of transfer (7.045%), projection (6.564%),
materialization (4.831%), literal cache sorting (0.866%) and rescan (0.006%).
They did not isolate comparator cost from sorting mechanics or prove a particular
replacement algorithm. P0.3 implemented no optimization; P1.1 subsequently added
the literal-only heap merge with shared authoritative ordering and retention.
The measurements above remain historical evidence, not current performance claims.

## Profiling cleanup

On 2026-10-14, the temporary feature, executable target, private timer/runner
modules and all phase-boundary instrumentation were removed. The P1.1 heap merge,
shared authoritative comparator/retention and P2.1 old-source check removal remain.
This report retains the original phase measurements and limitations; it is not
an active execution guide. Existing Criterion benchmarks remain unchanged.
